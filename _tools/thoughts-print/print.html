When your thoughts won't stop

You don't have to force your mind quiet. Take one small step at a time, with Him. Each step is backed by published research and paired with Scripture.

“From the end of the earth, I will call to you when my heart is overwhelmed. Lead me to the rock that is higher than I.”Psalm 61:2 (WEBU)

1

One long breath out

Breathe in through your nose. At the top, take one more short sip of air. Then let it all out slowly through your mouth, longer than the breath in. Repeat three to five times. If you feel dizzy, stop and breathe normally.

Five minutes a day of this “cyclic sighing” lifted mood more than mindfulness meditation in a randomized trial [1].

2

Name the feeling

One word, said quietly to God: “Lord, I feel                   .”

“Search me, God, and know my heart. Try me, and know my thoughts.”Psalm 139:23 (WEBU)

Putting a feeling into words calmed the brain's alarm center (the amygdala) in a brain-imaging study [2].

3

Step back from the loudest thought

Put six words in front of it: “I am having the thought that                               .” It's a thought, not the final word.

“bringing every thought into captivity to the obedience of Christ,”from 2 Corinthians 10:5 (WEBU)

This made negative self-statements less upsetting in a small experiment [3].

4

Speak to your soul

Talk to yourself by name, the way you'd talk to a friend: “            , you are not alone in this. You can hope in God.”

“Why are you in despair, my soul? Why are you disturbed within me? Hope in God! For I shall still praise him for the saving help of his presence.”Psalm 42:5 (WEBU)

Using your own name or “you,” instead of “I,” lowered distress under stress [4].

5

Give the worry an appointment

Pick a time later today, no more than 30 minutes, to think it through and pray. Until then: “Not now. Later.”  My time:               

“Therefore don’t be anxious for tomorrow, for tomorrow will be anxious for itself. Each day’s own evil is sufficient.”Matthew 6:34 (WEBU)

Two weeks of a set daily worry period reduced worry, anxiety and insomnia in a preliminary randomized study [5].

6

Write it down, then hand it over

Everything that's spinning, no order needed. Then read it once as a prayer.

“casting all your worries on him, because he cares for you.”1 Peter 5:7 (WEBU)

Writing about worries may “offload” them from working memory [6].

7

Listen, look, and rest

Step outside or to a window, or play water sounds. For one minute, just listen and look at what He made.

“He makes me lie down in green pastures. He leads me beside still waters.”Psalm 23:2 (WEBU)

Nature sounds are linked to faster stress recovery and lower stress [7, 8], and a walk in nature reduced rumination [9].

You brought it to Him. Your thoughts may still be moving, and that's okay.
“You will keep whoever’s mind is steadfast in perfect peace, because he trusts in you.”Isaiah 26:3 (WEBU)

Left out on purpose: the “5-4-3-2-1” senses exercise (we found no study testing it on its own) and binaural beats (a 2023 systematic review found inconsistent evidence: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10198548/).
